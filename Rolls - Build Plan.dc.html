1 · The one decision that shapes everything

You asked for a synced account and said it must work offline. Those two together dictate the order of work, and it is not the order you might expect: local-first, sync second.

An app that works offline cannot treat the server as the source of truth — the phone has to be. So the correct build is a complete local app that happens to sync, not a cloud app with a cache bolted on. This is lucky, because it also matches your "usable for me, fastest" bias: the local app is the part that makes Rolls usable, and it comes first.

Consequence

If the credits run out halfway through this plan, you still end up with a real, installed, offline logbook holding your actual rolls. Sync is an upgrade to that, not a prerequisite for it. Nothing here is wasted work.

3 · Goals

Short term — the finish line for these credits

Rolls installed on your iPhone home screen and usable in the dark at a café with no signal: you load a roll, log frames as you shoot, attach photos, and nothing is ever lost. Your real rolls, not demo ones. A backup you can export.

Long term — after the credits

Accounts with real sign-in and multi-device sync · photos synced to cloud storage · real map tiles with GPS capture · then, if you still want it, a native build for the App Store and Play Store from the repo.

4 · The plan, phase by phase

Each phase ends with the app in a coherent, usable state. Phases 1–4 are the usable-app core; 5–7 are the upgrades.

Clear the decks

Phase 0 · small

Remove the demo rolls so the app starts empty with the first-run state that already exists. Make the Shelf, Cosmos and stats all read from an empty library without breaking.

Outcome: a clean, honest starting point for your real data.

One source of truth, and it persists

Phase 1 · the keystone

Build a single store module that owns everything: rolls, every frame's settings, custom light conditions, profile, default film, units, theme and appearance. Both screens read from it and write to it; the Cosmos derives its calendar and map from it. It saves to the device automatically on every change and reloads on open.

  • Frame data moves out of the logging screen and into the store, so an edit to frame 12 immediately shows on the Shelf and in the Cosmos.
  • Settings become real: default film pre-fills new rolls, units drive the focus-distance display, custom light conditions survive restarts.
  • Every screen keeps its current design exactly — this is plumbing, not redesign.
Stop pointThe app remembers everything. You can log a roll, close the app, come back tomorrow and continue. This is the single biggest step from prototype to tool.

Photos that survive

Phase 2

Store reference photos and scans durably on the device rather than in memory, downscaling on import so a roll of 36 scans does not fill your phone. Scan matching writes into the store, so matched scans appear on their frames permanently.

Outcome: full photo support — reference shots and scans, kept.

Installed and offline

Phase 3 · the usable finish line

Turn Rolls into an installable app: an app manifest, the roll-canister icon at every size, and a service worker that caches the app so it opens and runs with no signal. Then you add it to your iPhone home screen and it behaves like an app — full screen, no browser chrome, works on a plane.

Stop pointThis is "usable for me". Rolls is on your phone, holds your real rolls, and works offline. If the credits end here, you have the app.

Backup and export

Phase 4 · cheap insurance

Make Export library real: your whole library out as a single file (all rolls, frames and photos) that you can keep, plus re-import to restore. Small job, disproportionate value — it is your safety net until sync exists, and your escape hatch forever.

Outcome: your data is yours, and recoverable.

Real accounts and sync

Phase 5 · the upgrade you asked for

Wire the finished Auth screens to real sign-up, sign-in and password reset, then sync the library: local changes queue up and push when you have signal, and signing in on another device pulls everything down. Photos stay local at this stage; the settings data syncs first because it is small, fast and the part you would hate to lose.

  • Requires a Supabase project — see §6.
  • Conflict handling is last-write-wins per record, which is correct and sufficient for one person on two devices.
Stop pointYour stated target: sign in anywhere, your rolls are there.

Photos in the cloud

Phase 6

Upload photos in the background to private cloud storage and pull them on other devices, keeping the local copy as the fast path. Deliberately after metadata sync: images are the slowest, largest and most expensive part, and the app is fully usable without them syncing.

Real map and GPS

Phase 7

Replace the stylized map with real map tiles behind the existing Cosmos zoom continuum, and capture real coordinates when you log a frame, with manual override. The Calendar → Map → Globe feel is preserved; only the map surface becomes real.

  • Requires a free tile API key — see §6.
  • Kept last on purpose: it is the most visually satisfying item and the least necessary for logging film.

5 · What I recommend for the credits expiring tomorrow

Do Phases 0 → 4 in one run, in that order, and stop. That sequence is self-contained, has no external dependencies, and ends with an installed offline app holding your real rolls plus a backup file. It is the highest ratio of "actually usable" to "credits spent".

Start Phase 5 only if there is clearly time left and your Supabase project already exists. Sync is the one phase that can half-land badly — a broken sync is worse than no sync — so it should never be the thing that runs out of credits mid-way.

Sequencing rule

Never begin a phase we cannot finish. Each phase above is a clean commit point; half a phase is technical debt you will pay for later at full price.

7 · Risks, stated plainly

RiskMitigation
iPhone can evict a web app's stored data if the app goes unused for weeksPhase 4 export, then Phase 5 sync. This is the main reason backup comes before sync.
Scans are large; phone storage limits are realDownscale on import in Phase 2; keep your full-resolution originals on your computer. Rolls is a logbook, not an archive.
Sync conflicts across two devicesLast-write-wins per record with timestamps. Adequate for a single user; revisit only if you ever share a library.
Credits run out mid-phaseThe phase ordering above. Every phase boundary is a working app.
An installed web app is not an App Store appTrue, and out of scope for these credits. It looks and behaves like an app on your home screen; a native build is a later, separate project from the repo.

8 · Priorities, from your answers

You named frame-logging accuracy, the dial feel, the Cosmos calendar, the map and globe, scan matching, themes, and export as what you would miss. All but the real map are already designed and working; this plan makes them durable, which is the thing standing between them and daily use. Export moves earlier than it otherwise would because you named it — it lands in Phase 4 rather than being an afterthought.

The map and globe are the one priority I am deliberately scheduling last, because logging film does not depend on them and they need an external key. If you would rather have the real map than cloud photo sync, say so and I will swap Phases 6 and 7.
